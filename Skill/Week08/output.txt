====================================================================================
PART 2  WHY DIVIDE BY sqrt(d_k)
====================================================================================

Q and K are random with unit variance, so each dot product is a sum
of d_k independent products and its variance grows as d_k. Large
scores saturate softmax: one weight goes to 1, the rest to 0, and the
gradient through softmax vanishes.

   d_k |   std(raw) |  std(scaled) |  max w unscaled |  max w scaled
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
     4 |      2.054 |        1.027 |          0.9711 |        0.5854
    16 |      3.963 |        0.991 |          0.9708 |        0.2991
    64 |      7.906 |        0.988 |          1.0000 |        0.2072
   256 |     16.416 |        1.026 |          1.0000 |        0.2887
  1024 |     32.003 |        1.000 |          1.0000 |        0.3595

  std(raw) tracks sqrt(d_k); std(scaled) stays near 1 at every size.
  Unscaled, the largest weight approaches 1.0 as d_k grows - a hard
  argmax with no usable gradient. Scaled, it stays moderate.

====================================================================================
PART 7  WHY LONG CONTEXT IS EXPENSIVE
====================================================================================

The score matrix is L x L, so both memory and compute grow with L^2.
This single fact drives Flash Attention, sliding windows and every
other long-context trick.

  seq len |     score matrix |    fp32 memory |   vs L=512
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
      128 |        128 x 128 |         0.1 MB |          -
      512 |        512 x 512 |         1.0 MB |         1x
     2048 |      2048 x 2048 |        16.0 MB |        16x
     8192 |      8192 x 8192 |       256.0 MB |       256x
    32768 |    32768 x 32768 |      4096.0 MB |      4096x

  Doubling the context quadruples the attention matrix. At 32k tokens
  a single head's scores need 4 GB in fp32 - per layer, per batch item.
